1. How to Build Your Price Book in 20 Minutes

  1. Pull Your Last 3 Grocery Receipts: Highlight the items you buy every single week or month—proteins, dairy, cooking oils, coffee, paper towels, trash bags, and kids' snacks. Ignore one-off impulse buys.
  2. Convert Every Price to a Single Unit: Never compare a 14.5-oz box against an 18-oz box by sticker price. Divide price by ounces, pounds, or sheets so every store competes on an even playing field.
  3. Establish Your "Stock-Up Buy Price": Once you log 3 to 4 weeks of ad flyers from two competing stores (e.g., Aldi vs. Kroger or Walmart vs. Sam's Club), circle the lowest unit price. Whenever an item hits or beats that number, buy enough to last 6 to 8 weeks until the next sale cycle.
  4. Combine Sale Cycles With Digital Rebates: Stack your stock-up price with cash-back apps and discounted store credit—see our guide on How to Stretch Your Grocery Budget With Discounted Gift Cards to shave another 5% to 12% off at checkout.

Grocery Price Book FAQ

Should I include sales tax in my price book?

No. Always record the pre-tax shelf or sale price so you can accurately compare unit prices across neighboring towns or states with different grocery tax rates.

Are warehouse clubs like Costco and Sam's Club always cheaper per unit?

Not always. Warehouse clubs typically beat standard supermarket everyday prices by 15% to 25%, but local grocery store loss-leader front-page sales combined with digital coupons frequently beat warehouse club unit prices on meat, cereal, and personal care items.
